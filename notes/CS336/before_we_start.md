# Before we start: why I can't start on CS336
Well I thought I could start learning "Language Modeling from Scratch" with CS336 courses as the name itself sort of implies that we can do it ... well... _"from scratch"_?

But instead it is more of like starting "from scratch" for the starter code part, not the skills part. That is, tons of prerequisites for programming, maths and machine learning...which I basically knew nothing about. Well, I do know bits of python here and there but obviously that's far from enough.

I've been asking my mentor for help and he suggests that I can have GPT generate a study plan for me. Indeed, _nothing's impossible to learn these days_.

So here I am, equipped with AI generated study plan for the semester (and tons of comforts that it's totally acceptable for a freshman to not know liner algebra), and suddenly realized that I have to at least study 4-6 months to start on CS336.

At least I still have time to take everything slowly.

But perhaps I don't.

From the first day at school we're told that research is super-ultra important and there are people emailing professors to join the team at freshman fall. I mean _what ?!_ We're basically high-school graduates how can we possibly know what to do in a lab? Shouldn't we be focusing on basic course work instead? As undergraduates, shouldn't we first build our basic skill set and then try to actually achieve something in doing research projects by using what we've learned?

But to be honest I was sort of intoxicated by this toxic noise at first. After all we just escaped from the _Gaokao_ system and people are still stressed. Thank god that my mentor talked me into senses and started finding my critical thinking skills back. He showed me the correct route to actually learn stuff from research experiences: at least first learn something and then apply what you have learned.

So, I tries learn the CS336 courses recommended by my mentor and came to here.

Well, due to the quick pace of GC (that is to say, you have to achieve and present something as during sophomore year instead of being able to relax till senior, sort of like mid-term election), I'm still in some sort of rush here, trying to learn stuff before we actually get started on those in school.

But at least everything seems working, and hopefully everything will work out just fine.

Anyways, here's the study plan generated by ChatGPT, I'm posting it here to encourage myself to finish everything on time, and to hopefully find someone willing to study with me too!

## How I’ll Study

1. Read the lesson and run small examples.
2. Work out the algorithm before writing code.
3. Attempt exercises independently, then ask for hints when needed.
4. Test ordinary cases and boundary cases.
5. Record what I learned and what remains confusing.

I’ll take a short break during Saturday’s session. If a topic needs more practice, I’ll continue it in the next session and adjust the schedule.
